    Two paths through the system

    Every part belongs to one of two paths. Whether the user is waiting decides how fast each path must be. Think of a restaurant: the waiter serves the plate right away, and the manager reads the reviews in the evening.

    Hot path · the user is waiting

    Serve Sara her answer

    Sara clicks → evaluate() → app calls its LLM → Sara sees the subject → track()

    Our share of the wait: under 1 ms. evaluate() reads memory and track() drops a note in a queue. No network calls and no judging here.

    Background · nobody is waiting

    Judge, decide, adjust

    queue → worker → judge LLM → PostgreSQL → controller → new config → SDKs

    It can take seconds or minutes, and that's fine. A rollback reaches every app within about 30 s, the SDK's refresh interval.

    One rollout, start to finish

    The same machinery, seen over days instead of milliseconds. Two prompt versions, two endings.

    Prompt v2 · a good version
    shadow 0% seen→ 1% 2 h→ 5% 6 h→ 25% 24 h→ 50% 24 h→ 100% ✓
    Prompt v3 · deliberately bad: "answer in capitals, max 2 words"
    shadow 0% seen→ 1% 2 h→ 5% quality falling→ rollback → 0% · Slack alert · 1 h cooldown

    At every arrow the controller checks the numbers: enough samples, and is the experimental version statistically no worse than the baseline? Only then does it advance. Because v3 was stopped at 5%, at most 1 user in 20 ever saw it.
